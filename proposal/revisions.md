Add to possible case studies section:

Malawi: mWater has been used to map and assess the quality of  approximately 47,000 water points serving 4.8 million people with data captured on the platform and accessible openly

Cape Town, South Africa:  - The City publishes weekly dam levels for the Western Cape Water Supply System on its open data portal, including downloadable historical time series; A live public dashboard at waterdashboard.co.za shows current storage, historical trends, and 24-month projections; The data is openly licensed and accessible via ArcGIS, meaning third parties can build on it — which they have (capetowndamlevels.co.za is one independent site built on the same data)

Kampala, Uganda: Weyonje ("emptying" in Luganda) is a GIS-enabled mobile app developed by the Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA) to manage faecal sludge management across the city. It was built with GSMA Digital Utilities Innovation Fund support from 2017, in partnership with GIZ and the National Water and Sewerage Corporation.

Finland:  VEETI is Finland's national Water Services Information System, operational since 2016, owned by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry and operated by the Finnish Environment Institute (SYKE). It is essentially a compulsory national database for all water and wastewater utilities in the

Country. Non-restricted data from VEETI is publicly accessible via open APIs through SYKE's environmental data services, meaning researchers, planners, and third-party developers can query it directly.
