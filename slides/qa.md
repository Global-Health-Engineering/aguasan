# Clarification Q&A for the 3.5-minute window

Consolidated from the five variants' critique files, deduplicated, and
cut to the ten questions most likely in this room (organizers, the
three other proposal teams, SDC/RésEAU voices, community veterans).
Two-sentence answers, written to be spoken.

## Top 10 questions

1. How would this combine with the digital-tools proposal?

   Gladly, and we would say yes on the spot: they strengthen how data
   is collected, we make sure collected data ends up published, so a
   combined week walks data along one collection-to-publication value
   chain instead of splitting into two topics. We would co-design the
   pre-work so their tools feed the datasets participants bring, and
   publish the week's tool inventory itself as a data package with a
   DOI.

2. Why not merge this with the strategic proposals (polycrisis, after
   the aid era)?

   Their output is another document, valuable but read and shelved,
   while this week sends people home with published artifacts and
   durable skills. The diagnosis can open our week as the keynote and
   the workforce questions can close it, so the committee gets both
   without trading away the working format.

3. Are 30 to 50 packages realistic, and are they really published by
   Friday?

   Realistic because the dataset is the entry ticket, screened at
   signup, and pre-work starts at least four weeks before arrival with
   accounts, permissions, and documentation settled, while AI agents
   absorb the repetitive file and documentation work. By Friday the
   packages exist in documented draft form on shared infrastructure;
   archiving with a DOI is the defined final step, supported after the
   week.

4. Do participants need to code, and does qualitative or sensitive
   data qualify?

   Nobody arrives as a coder: a shared cloud environment gives
   everyone the same starting point on free and open tools, and access
   to AI tooling for those who cannot pay for licenses is worked out
   together as part of the retreat agenda. Datasets are screened at
   signup, and our FAIRqual experience covers qualitative and
   sensitive material while protecting research subjects.

5. The ETH funding window closes in 2026; who guarantees the
   follow-through?

   A permanent full-time position at ETH Zurich manages the
   openwashdata community independent of grant cycles, and we
   committed to every custodian contribution in the template, from the
   October 2026 preparatory event through the Steering Committee to
   the free third course. The monitoring behind these numbers
   continues long-term, so the workshop's effect stays measurable and
   becomes the baseline for the next funding applications.

6. Is this not ETH securing its own funding continuity?

   The outputs belong to the participants and the sector: published
   data packages, durable skills, and a free follow-up course, while
   AGUASAN funds the room and the reach, not the R&D, which is already
   paid for. The funding lines we named finance the sector's open-data
   work, and an AGUASAN mandate documents sector demand rather than
   channelling money to ETH.

7. Is Alex a real person, and is one story enough to justify a topic?

   Alex is openly a composite drawn from the practitioners in our two
   course cohorts, and every sector-level number behind her story is
   computed live from our published washopenresearch package. The
   demand evidence stands apart from the story: over 200 and then over
   150 course registrations, 70 capstone projects, and a data steward
   embedded in a partner organisation in Malawi.

8. Can you show that these skills actually keep people employable?

   Honestly: the claim rests on the transferability of the toolset,
   not on an outcome statistic; Git, GitHub, Quarto, and safe agent
   work are the working methods of the wider data economy, inside and
   outside WASH. The alumni of both cohorts are reachable today for
   what they have produced since, and the long-term monitoring
   strategy is how we will keep answering this question with evidence.

9. Why does PLOS Water show only 333 articles, and is that comparison
   fair?

   PLOS Water started publishing in 2022, and the chart counts its
   research articles only, 333 of 436, because opinions, reviews, and
   editorials have no data to share; the label and caption disclose
   both. The gap is the finding, not a confound: same sector, same
   article type, and the one structural difference is a journal that
   asks for the statement and supports its authors, which is exactly
   the mechanism the retreat applies, and 333 articles is an ample
   sample for a share estimate.

10. If data reaches a repository, is the problem solved?

    No, and we measured it: we followed every repository link in the
    corpus this week, roughly one in five is already dead, and of the
    deposits we could open through platform APIs, about two thirds hold
    no machine-readable file and more than half carry no documentation.
    These counts are days old and await our human validation pass, so
    treat them as approximate; the point stands that a mandate can move
    data into a repository, but only skills make it usable there, which
    is exactly what the retreat teaches.

## Women's-network concern (Stronger Together), prepared answer

If the impact-on-women concern from the after-the-aid-era proposal's
demand evidence comes up:

The Stronger Together concern is about representation and recognition.
Our answer is concrete on both: a speaker lineup that prioritizes women
in WASH data, and publication credit itself, because a citable data
package with a DOI turns the data work women already do in programmes
into recognized, citable output. Add the access commitments, remote
participation without travel and AI tooling for those who cannot pay,
and participation barriers drop as well.

## The combination test, prepared answer

The organizers will explicitly test combining the two
practice-oriented proposals and, separately, the two strategic ones.

With the digital-tools proposal: say yes first, then shape it. The two
proposals are one value chain: they collect, we publish, so a combined
week runs collection to citable publication as a single arc rather
than a broader, thinner topic. Concretely: co-designed pre-work so
their tools feed the datasets participants bring, one shared GitHub
infrastructure so both halves land in the same place, and the week's
digital tool inventory itself published as a documented data package
with a DOI, a live demonstration of the practice we teach. Publishing
stays the spine of the week: the exit condition remains that every
participant leaves having published, not only having discussed.

Against a strategic-proposals merger: their output is another
document, a synthesis the sector reads; this week produces artifacts
and durable skills, 30 to 50 published data packages and a toolchain
each participant keeps. The integration moves let the committee have
both without a merger: the polycrisis diagnosis opens the week as the
keynote, the RWSN workforce questions close it as the reflection
track, and hands-on publishing fills the days in between.
